You probably recognize your company here.

  • The founders, the sales team and the product team each tell a slightly different story, and no one owns the connection between them and the market.
  • You need traction now, and a full-time CMO is either more than the company needs yet or too slow to hire.
  • You need several plates spinning at once (brand, organic, social, paid, events, email, the CRM) and one person who can run each of them front to back.
  • A past agency drifted off the scope you hired it for, or you’d rather work with one senior person directly than through a recruiter or an agency.
  • You want someone who will learn your business quickly and start launching and shipping, not spend a quarter on a deck.
  • Marketing at your company is one person or no one, and the stage you’re at doesn’t justify a full team yet.

What “marketing team of one” means in practice.

I can work across the whole stack: positioning and brand, organic and content, social, paid media, events, email, and the CRM underneath all of it. I can run each one front to back, which is different from being the deepest specialist in any of them, and I don’t over-index on a channel because I like it. They get funded in the order your pipeline needs them.

I’m upfront from the first call about which parts of your scope I can handle myself and where it’s more efficient and more impactful to bring in another resource: an agency, a freelancer, a hire. If the honest answer is that you need a specialist, I’ll say so.

Questions people ask before they book.

What does a fractional CMO cost?

Scoped projects start at $10,000. Ongoing advisory starts at $10,000 a month, month-to-month. Embedded work, where I operate as part of your team, starts at $20,000 a month with a longer-term commitment.

For comparison, the median base salary for a Director or Head of Marketing at a B2B startup is about $225,000 in the latest MKT1 job-post analysis. That is roughly $18,750 a month before benefits, equity, recruiter fees or the months it takes to hire and ramp someone.

How is a fractional CMO different from a marketing agency?

With an agency you buy a team and a scope. With me you get one senior person who plans the work and does it: strategy, copy, email, CRM, campaigns, events. When a piece of the plan is better run by an agency, I’ll say so and then manage that agency for you.

Can one person really cover brand, organic, social, paid media and events?

I can run each of them front to back, and I’m good enough at all of them to keep several programs moving at once. I’m not the deepest specialist in any one. If your paid spend needs a full-time specialist, or your social calendar needs daily production, I’ll tell you that in discovery and help you figure out whether an agency, a freelancer or a hire is the better fit.

What’s the difference between advisory and embedded?

In advisory, I sit just outside the business. I work directly with one or two people, we meet once or twice a week, and most of the work happens asynchronously. Embedded means you treat me like part of the team, and you get as much of me as you need. We pick the starting point in discovery, and many engagements shift from one to the other.

Do you work through recruiters or agencies?

No. You hire me directly. There’s no recruiter, no placement fee and no agency layer between you and the person doing the work.

How fast can you get up to speed on our business?

Quickly, because I spend the first stretch on your customers, your ICP, how deals are actually won and lost, and what your sales and product teams are hearing. I would rather ship a few obviously useful things early than hand you a long strategy deck and disappear for a month.

Do you work with our sales and product teams?

Yes, closely. The best marketing I’ve done happened with the wall between marketing and sales down far enough that I could see how deals were won and lost. Product and customer success get the same treatment. Marketing makes bad calls when it sits too far from whoever hears what customers actually say.

What’s the commitment?

Advisory is month-to-month, and you can cancel or pause any time. Embedded engagements are a longer-term commitment, scoped in discovery.

How many clients do you take on at once?

A handful. Proximity is a small practice by design, so the person you meet on the first call is the person doing the work.

Tell me what marketing looks like at your company right now.

Twenty minutes. Bring what’s actually true, including what’s missing.
