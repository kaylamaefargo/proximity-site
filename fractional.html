What a fractional CMO does, and how I work.

A fractional CMO is a senior marketing leader who works with your company on a flexible basis, owning strategy and results without a full-time executive’s cost. I work at two altitudes: as the hands-on operator who does the work, and as the leader who sets the plan, hires the team and manages the agencies. Most companies need some of each, and the mix changes as they grow.

Go-to-market strategy & positioning

Where the company is, where it’s trying to get to, and what has to be true about the market’s understanding of you to close that gap.

Product marketing & sales enablement

Positioning, launch plans, and sales enablement that actually gets used on calls, not left in a shared drive nobody opens after the kickoff.

Demand generation

The channels worth the spend, and just as useful to know, the ones you should stop funding.

Brand & content

A brand overhaul doesn’t have to take a year. I ran one in eight weeks at a mid-scale-up, without stopping everything else the team was doing.

Marketing ops & the CRM

HubSpot set up properly, lead-to-deal ownership between marketing and sales, and the sequences and reporting that sit on top.

Building the team

You probably recognize your company here.

  • The founders, the sales team and the product team each tell a slightly different story, and no one owns the connection between them and the market.
  • You need traction now, and a full-time CMO is either more than the company needs yet or too slow to hire.
  • You need several plates spinning at once (brand, organic, social, paid, events, email, the CRM) and one person who can run each of them front to back.
  • A past agency drifted off the scope you hired it for, or you’d rather work with one senior person directly than through a recruiter or an agency.
  • You want someone who will learn your business quickly and start launching and shipping, not spend a quarter on a deck.
  • You have execution help, like an agency or a junior hire, but no senior direction, and the founder is still the bottleneck.

What “marketing team of one” means in practice.

I can work across the whole stack: positioning and brand, organic and content, social, paid media, events, email, and the CRM underneath all of it. I can run each one front to back, which is different from being the deepest specialist in any of them, and I don’t over-index on a channel because I like it. They get funded in the order your pipeline needs them.

I’m upfront from the first call about which parts of your scope I can handle myself and where it’s more efficient and more impactful to bring in another resource: an agency, a freelancer, a hire. If the honest answer is that you need a specialist, I’ll say so.

Questions people ask before they book.

What is a fractional CMO?

A fractional CMO is a senior marketing leader who works with your company on a flexible, part-time basis, owning strategy and results without the cost of a full-time executive. In my practice that ranges from a hands-on marketing team of one to an embedded leader running the whole function. You pick the altitude.

What does a fractional CMO actually do?

Go-to-market strategy and positioning, product marketing and sales enablement, demand generation across channels, brand and content, marketing operations and the CRM underneath them, and building and managing the team and agencies around the plan. How much of that I do myself and how much I direct depends on the altitude we agree on in discovery.

When should a company hire a fractional CMO?

Usually when a few of these are true: the founders still own most marketing decisions, sales, product and marketing describe the buyer in different words, marketing activity is up but pipeline isn’t clearer, you have execution help (an agency, a freelancer, a junior hire) but no senior direction, or you’re heading into a launch or a raise and need a plan before you spend more. It’s also the right call when a full-time CMO is more than the company needs yet.

Fractional CMO vs. full-time CMO: which do I need?

A fractional CMO fits when you need senior leadership and accountability without a full-time executive’s cost, ramp time and long-term commitment. If marketing genuinely needs a full-time senior leader, I’ll tell you that in discovery and help you scope the hire.

How is a fractional CMO different from a marketing agency?

With an agency you buy a team and a scope. With me you get one senior person who plans the work and does it, or directs it: strategy, copy, email, CRM, campaigns, events. When a piece of the plan is better run by an agency, I’ll say so and then manage that agency for you.

How is a fractional CMO different from a marketing consultant?

A consultant typically hands you a plan and leaves. I stay for the doing: I own the plan, run or direct the execution, and answer for the result. If all you need is a plan or a diagnosis, a scoped project, like the Revenue Chain Teardown, is the better shape.

What does a fractional CMO cost?

Scoped projects start at $10,000. Ongoing advisory starts at $8,000 a month, month-to-month. Embedded work, where I operate as part of your team, starts at $15,000 a month with a longer-term commitment.

For comparison, the median base salary for a Director or Head of Marketing at a B2B startup is about $225,000 in the latest MKT1 job-post analysis. That is roughly $18,750 a month before benefits, equity, recruiter fees or the months it takes to hire and ramp someone.

What’s the difference between advisory and embedded?

In advisory, I sit just outside the business. I work directly with one or two people, we meet once or twice a week, and most of the work happens asynchronously. Embedded means you treat me like part of the team, and you get as much of me as you need. We pick the starting point in discovery, and many engagements shift from one to the other.

Can one person really cover brand, organic, social, paid media and events?

I can run each of them front to back, and I’m good enough at all of them to keep several programs moving at once. I’m not the deepest specialist in any one. If your paid spend needs a full-time specialist, or your social calendar needs daily production, I’ll tell you that in discovery and help you figure out whether an agency, a freelancer or a hire is the better fit.

What do the first 90 days look like?

The first month goes to learning: your customers, your ICP, how deals are actually won and lost, and what your sales and product teams are hearing. I ship a few obviously useful things early. The second month turns that into a plan and a shared story across sales, product and marketing. By day 90 you should have a clear roadmap and a working rhythm. I don’t promise revenue outcomes before I’ve done the diagnosis.

How long until we see results?

Clarity comes in weeks: sharper positioning, a plan the team agrees on, a clearer picture of what to stop doing. Pipeline impact depends on your sales cycle. I’ll tell you what to expect after discovery, not before.

Do you work through recruiters or agencies?

No. You hire me directly. There’s no recruiter, no placement fee and no agency layer between you and the person doing the work.

What kinds of companies do you work with?

B2B companies, from pre-revenue startups to enterprises doing nine figures in revenue. I’ve been a company’s first marketing hire, its entire marketing department, and the leader of teams of twenty-plus. What they have in common is a B2B sales motion that needs a clearer story, a steadier pipeline, or a function that doesn’t exist yet.

Can you run marketing ops and the CRM, like HubSpot?

Yes, hands-on. I’ve implemented HubSpot, rebuilt lead-to-deal ownership and SLAs between marketing and sales, and built the sequences on top. If you need a full-time ops specialist, I’ll say so.

Do you work with our sales and product teams?

Yes, closely. The best marketing I’ve done happened with the wall between marketing and sales down far enough that I could see how deals were won and lost. Product and customer success get the same treatment. Marketing makes bad calls when it sits too far from whoever hears what customers actually say.

What’s the commitment, and how do we end it?

Advisory is month-to-month, and you can cancel or pause any time. Embedded engagements are a longer-term commitment, scoped in discovery.

How many clients do you take on at once?

A handful. Proximity is a small practice by design, so the person you meet on the first call is the person doing the work.

What should I ask before hiring any fractional CMO?

How they’d spend the first 30, 60 and 90 days. How many clients they work with at once. Who they’ve worked with at your stage, and whether you can talk to them. How scope is written down, including what’s owned, what’s advisory and what’s excluded. What happens if either side wants out. I’m glad to answer all of them on a first call.
